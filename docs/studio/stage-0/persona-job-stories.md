# Stage 0 — Review-board job stories

These are simulated design perspectives specified in the brief, not interviews or endorsements by real people.

## Sheikha — family office principal

“When I consider a family address in Saadiyat or the Palm, I want to speak naturally in Arabic and see only a few relevant places, so I can judge them without exposing my family’s plans. Portals ask me for my number before earning my trust.” Start at Arabic Arrival → speak/type a beachfront intent → inspect evidence → request a private introduction. Success: no public sharing, no exposed private units, a Gulf-friendly voice with no sales pressure.

## Karim — founder abroad

“When I plan a summer home and a Dubai investment from London, I want one view of currencies, completion and payment commitments, so I can compare cash demands rather than attractive headline prices. Portals mix currencies and bury instalments.” Start at mixed-market intent → map comparison → GBP/USD presentation → dated static FX basis → payment timeline. Success: no implied live rates or invented schedules.

## Elena — relocating executive

“When I know little about Dubai, I want a patient spatial explanation of neighbourhoods and schools, so I can describe the family life I want before choosing a building. Portals make me choose an area I do not understand.” Start at “Explain Dubai for a family” → map-guided story → school layer → explain trade-offs. Success: the map teaches; demo POIs and travel estimates are unmistakable.

## Rajiv — investor

“When I assess an address, I want its price basis, area comparison, delivery expectation and evidence together, so I can identify what needs diligence. Portals substitute adjectives and unsupported yield claims for evidence.” Start at recommendation → numbers → source/date → comparison. Success: every figure has provenance and estimates expose assumptions.

## Head of Sales — developer

“When I introduce a flagship project, I want Darivo to communicate its place and architecture and surface qualified demand, so I receive informed introductions rather than unqualified form fills. Portals commoditise the project and imply inventory they do not control.” Start at text wordmark → Showcase → Launch Room → private RSVP. Success: no copied renders, no fabricated track record and no demo units attached to real projects.

## Private Advisor

“When a client hands over a conversation, I want their intent, uncertainties and selected addresses in one context, so I can respond without making them repeat themselves. Portals give me a contact record without the reasoning.” Start at handoff queue → brief and summary → scoped suggestion → viewing confirmation. Success: private notes remain staff-only; actions are audited.

## The Critic

“When I open Darivo, I want a spatial conversation with a coherent visual idea, so I immediately understand why it exists. I am tired of dark property cards presented as innovation.” Start at Arrival → type intent → map response → project story. Success: one uninterrupted idea, deliberate Arabic typography, no ornamental motion or fictitious metrics.
