## A — Noir Cinematic

“I would take a meeting after seeing this opening. I would not yet commit my launch inventory. It understands discretion and atmosphere; I still need to see how that atmosphere turns into a qualified conversation.”

### Three delights

1. [Desktop Home](../media/v1/a-home-en-dark-1440.png): the property office feels private and geographically grounded. No competing listings, discount labels or invented developer logos dilute the first impression.
2. [Desktop Map](../media/v1/a-map-en-dark-1440.png): the single selected residence receives space and an individual point of view. This is the right starting attitude for a flagship presentation.
3. [Arabic tablet Map](../media/v1/a-map-ar-light-1024.png): the visible Demo badge and fictional-residence caption protect brand boundaries. The honest trade-off is more credible than a generic superlative.

### Three problems

1. [Desktop Map](../media/v1/a-map-en-dark-1440.png): “The Tidal House” has no source/date framework or track-record preview. The demo label is appropriate, but I cannot evaluate how a real developer's evidence will be respected from this card alone.
2. [Arabic mobile Home](../media/v1/a-home-ar-dark-390.png): the English Palm caption in an Arabic interface is precisely the kind of unfinished detail an Arabic-speaking principal would notice in a private launch.
3. The demonstrated closer-look action explains that the residence is a study; it does not preview a useful next step. The brief chips show taste, but not the budget/timing/consent context that would make an enquiry commercially useful. These are legitimate Stage 0 omissions, but they limit the current claim.

**Verdict:** “Lead with this level of atmosphere; prove the evidence and buyer qualification before asking for our name.”

## B — Sand Editorial

“I can imagine giving this to a client after a private meeting. It feels like someone has considered the residence rather than merely promoted it.”

### Three delights

1. [Desktop Home](../media/concept-b-v1-home-1440.png): the journal composition treats the coast as an asset worth understanding, which is a better foundation for a developer story than interchangeable hero photography.
2. [Desktop Map](../media/v1/b-map-en-light-1440.png): the two fictional projects have explicit demo price badges, a purpose and a limitation. The brand is not asking the buyer to confuse synthetic content with a real launch.
3. [Arabic tablet Home](../media/v1/b-home-ar-light-1024.png): the Arabic composition has air and dignity, and the composer is integrated into the reading column rather than added as a floating chatbot.

### Three problems

1. [Desktop Map](../media/v1/b-map-en-light-1440.png): the project items are more like catalogue notes than individual flagship identities. A developer would need richer place-specific narrative and bespoke launch art direction later.
2. [Desktop Home](../media/concept-b-v1-home-1440.png): “A different point of view” and “A considered life” are tasteful but general. They do not yet articulate why this service understands my development or my prospective buyer better than my own sales team does.
3. The visible “selection” action proves lightweight client interest, not qualification or consent to share. No sales outcome should be inferred from the saved count. Actual lead context and source freshness are still future evidence.

**Verdict:** “The best foundation for an evidence-led client dossier; add the singularity of a flagship launch without losing its restraint.”

## C — Glass Atlas, replacement v2

“Now I understand the commercial proposition: one relationship across two markets. That is more interesting than another website that says it sells expensive homes.”

### Three delights

1. [Desktop globe Home](../../../../prototypes/concept-c/reviews/home-v2.png): the two shores establish a clear cross-market story. My Egyptian buyer and my Dubai buyer can recognise a shared service without either market becoming a secondary menu item.
2. [Desktop Map](../media/v2/c-map-en-dark-1440.png): the visible Brief explains the relationship between client priorities and selected residences. This is the most convincing concept-level preview of informed demand, without pretending to be a real lead dashboard.
3. [Arabic tablet Home](../media/v2/c-home-ar-dark-1024.png): the composition gives Arabic and the two markets a deliberate editorial presence, and every residence remains identified as fictional demo content. No real developer is borrowed to manufacture credibility.

### Three problems

1. [Light mobile Home](../media/v2/c-home-en-light-390.png): the busy country labels directly behind the promise weaken the polish I would require for a private launch invitation.
2. [Arabic mobile Map](../media/v2/c-map-ar-light-390.png): North Coast is absent from the destination controls. A two-market proposition cannot lose Egypt when my client opens it on a phone.
3. [Desktop Map](../media/v2/c-map-en-dark-1440.png): three names share the same “Waterfront setting · family focus” rationale. Distinctive projects deserve distinctive reasons; otherwise the interface is still treating them as interchangeable inventory under an elegant surface.

**Verdict:** “This is the concept I would develop for a cross-market private launch, once the mobile journey and individual residence narratives earn the same care as the globe.”

## Commercial direction after all three

Choose C's two-market arrival, then use A's generous single-residence focus and B's explicit reasons and limitations when a buyer selects a place. The developer appeal score of nine is for this **Stage 0 concept direction**, not a claim that the unbuilt Launch Room or partner console has passed. Current mobile and label defects still prevent the overall stage gate.
