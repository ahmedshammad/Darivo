## Concept A — Noir Cinematic

### Three delights

1. “The dark desktop arrival has restraint. The large serif and quiet Palm camera frame feel like an invitation, not an agent’s advert.” (`a-home-en-dark-1440`)
2. “The Arabic headline is given real space on tablet; the composition begins from the right instead of squeezing a translation into the English line.” (`a-home-ar-dark-1024`)
3. “The residence tells me one compromise, and the Demo label is visible. That is a more respectful opening than superlatives.” (`a-map-ar-light-390`)

### Three problems

1. “Why is the largest place caption still ‘Palm Jumeirah’ in an otherwise Arabic arrival?” This initial untranslated caption is visible in `a-home-ar-dark-1024`; bilingual completeness needs correction.
2. “On my phone the residence sheet covers most of the useful coastline. I want to see what I am considering.” The fixed card in `a-map-ar-light-390` dominates the map with no evident collapsed state.
3. “The page says the conversation is mine alone, but this is still a concept promise. The displayed family/quiet/water brief also stays generic after a different request.” Chrome confirmed static brief chips after the mixed-market prompt; use accurately scoped privacy language and a visible responsive brief.

**Verdict:** “Keep this arrival’s composure, but earn my confidence through precise Arabic and a map I can actually see.”

## Concept B — Sand Editorial

### Three delights

1. “The Arabic cover is spacious and the invitation ‘صف لنا المكان اللي في بالك’ feels direct and warm.” (`b-home-ar-light-1440`)
2. “The privacy sentence distinguishes typed input from browser voice. That is more useful to me than an ornamental lock.” (`b-map-en-dark-1024`)
3. “I can keep a small private selection without contacting anyone. The mobile sheet has an explicit open control.” The save flow was exercised; `b-map-ar-light-390` shows the control.

### Three problems

1. “The slanted Arabic word ‘مكانها’ looks like an English styling decision.” The synthetic italic visible in `b-home-ar-light-1440` is not evidence of deliberate Arabic editorial typography.
2. “The mobile map puts the important land behind the sheet while I look at empty water.” (`b-map-ar-light-390`) The camera needs padding matched to the sheet height.
3. “An ‘Open your selection’ handle appears before the mobile Home headline, even though I have not started a selection.” (`b-home-en-dark-390`) This misplaced control weakens both privacy clarity and polish.

**Verdict:** “This feels the most like an understated private office, provided the Arabic display and mobile map composition receive another pass.”

## Concept C — Glass Atlas, revised globe arrival v2

This first comparison uses A/B capture set v1 and C capture set v2. The C redesign was supplied during the review; no claim is made that these are the same source revision. Additional evidence inspected: `media/v2/c-home-en-dark-1440.png`, `c-home-ar-dark-390.png`, `c-home-ar-light-1024.png`, `c-map-en-light-1440.png`, and `c-map-ar-light-390.png`. Elena’s teaching prompt was exercised in Chrome. The country label overlap and mobile panel coverage are visible, not speculative.

### Three delights

1. “The globe gives me both shores at once. It speaks to a family portfolio spanning countries rather than a single-city sales funnel.” (`media/v2/c-home-en-dark-1440.png`)
2. “The Arabic interface gives the brief a visible home and keeps the invitation calm.” (`media/v2/c-map-ar-light-390.png`)
3. “The fictional-project and approximate-location disclosures are specific. The interface also says nothing is saved in this prototype, rather than implying a secure account exists.” (`media/v2/c-map-en-light-1440.png`)

### Three problems

1. “Country names sit directly behind the Arabic headline and supporting lines. I have to separate two layers of text.” (`media/v2/c-home-ar-light-1024.png`) Labels must quiet down where the narrative lives.
2. “The Arabic italic emphasis still feels borrowed from Latin styling, and the small supporting copy is very small on my phone.” (`media/v2/c-home-ar-dark-390.png`) This needs an Arabic typography pass rather than a native-quality claim.
3. “On the mobile atlas the brief and residence panel leave almost no meaningful map. I cannot inspect my place without reading through a wall of panels.” (`media/v2/c-map-ar-light-390.png`)

**Verdict:** “The two-shore globe has the right ambition; quiet the language layers and give my phone back its map.”
