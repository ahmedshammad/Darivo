## Concept A — Noir Cinematic

### Three delights

1. “The map makes the product about places immediately; I am not staring at hundreds of rooms and prices.” (`a-home-en-dark-1440`)
2. “The North Coast is a first-class destination next to Palm and Saadiyat.” (`a-map-en-light-1440`)
3. “My full mixed-market sentence remains visible in the conversation when I submit it.” Chrome verified this; retaining my words is useful context.

### Three problems

1. “I asked for a North Coast summer home before 2028 and a Dubai comparison, but the brief still says ‘Family life / By the water / A little quieter’.” This is a concept interaction inconsistency, not a demand for production AI at Stage 0. It demonstrates canned personalization.
2. “The same ‘Tidal House’ now says North Coast while its compromise still talks about island life.” The Chrome flow showed the destination changing without the corresponding recommendation context. A static demo should still stay internally coherent.
3. “On mobile the map and its labels are largely hidden by the residence card. I lose the geographical context I came for.” (`a-map-ar-light-390`)

**Verdict:** “The map-led entry is promising, but the demonstration must preserve my two-market intent rather than quietly simplifying it.”

## Concept B — Sand Editorial

### Three delights

1. “Moving to Egypt changes the fictional selection and switches its native prices to EGP. The concept does not pretend those are converted AED prices.” Chrome observed Summer House / EGP 89M and Salt & Stone / EGP 74M, both Demo.
2. “Two considered options and a clear compromise are easier to start with than a result count.” (`b-map-en-dark-1024` and the exercised Egypt state)
3. “The Atlas and Journal remain connected. I can move from a general place story into a short selection.” The typed-brief and saved-selection flow was exercised.

### Three problems

1. “My mixed Egypt-and-Dubai request becomes only Egypt. There is no visible acknowledgment that half the intent is still unresolved.” The Stage 0 flow should say what it understood and what is still to discuss, even before real comparison is built.
2. “After the camera reaches Egypt, the field note still reads ‘25.2048° N’.” Chrome observed the static Dubai coordinate beside the Mediterranean heading. Editorial detail must be geographically coherent.
3. “The mobile cover uses four giant headline lines and puts the useful map below my first screen. The stray selection handle above it makes this feel unfinished.” (`b-home-en-dark-390`)

**Verdict:** “I prefer this clear, small selection, but make the map captions and the unresolved brief as precise as the typography.”

## Concept C — Glass Atlas, revised globe arrival v2

This first comparison uses A/B capture set v1 and C capture set v2. The C redesign was supplied during the review; no claim is made that these are the same source revision. Additional evidence inspected: `media/v2/c-home-en-dark-1440.png`, `c-home-ar-dark-390.png`, `c-home-ar-light-1024.png`, `c-map-en-light-1440.png`, and `c-map-ar-light-390.png`. Elena’s teaching prompt was exercised in Chrome. The country label overlap and mobile panel coverage are visible, not speculative.

### Three delights

1. “This is the only arrival that shows Egypt and the UAE as equal parts of my world before I ask.” (`media/v2/c-home-en-dark-1440.png`)
2. “The two regional cards make the choice concrete without pretending the markets are interchangeable.” The globe and regional labels provide a promising framework for the later cross-market comparison.
3. “A visible editable brief is the right contract: I should know what the office thinks I want.” (`media/v2/c-map-en-light-1440.png`)

### Three problems

1. “I can see every surrounding country name but the hero text becomes harder to read. An atlas does not need every label at this moment.” (`media/v2/c-home-ar-light-1024.png` and dark desktop arrival)
2. “The map state offers generic waterfront/family examples; it does not visibly retain a second market or a handover target.” This remains a concept-level interaction gap, not a requirement for completed payment tooling at Stage 0. The brief design should show unresolved intent rather than replace it with defaults.
3. “The mobile panel layout leaves the actual coastline behind content.” (`media/v2/c-map-ar-light-390.png`) A clear collapsed view is needed before this can be my main comparison surface.

**Verdict:** “Choose this globe as the arrival for a two-country office; pair it with a more precise brief and a less obstructed mobile atlas.”
