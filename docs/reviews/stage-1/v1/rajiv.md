## Three delights

1. The [full English guide](../media/v2/design-en-light-1440.png) treats a price, a comparison and its assumptions as one composition. The standalone real source example is clearly separated from fictional residence prices. A developer name is not being used to confer authenticity on a synthetic unit.
2. The [recorded currency checks](client-interactions.json) reconcile: AED20M becomes USD5,446,000 or EGP270M at the declared fictional rates. EGP54M +162M +54M equals the total, and the projection has a visible date and demo qualification. Original native property prices remain unchanged in the preference browser test.
3. The [native database run](../native-db.json) demonstrates 250 fictional units, 13 reference projects and zero invalid attachments. Missing retrieval dates remain missing. Tests enforce the boundary against both seeded and subsequently edited records; the legacy developer editor cannot silently rewrite a real reference.

## Three problems

1. The numeral preference changes monetary amounts and percentages but leaves authored areas and years unchanged. In the same evidence panel, `٢٠%` sits beside `2026`. Numerical consistency is part of trust; this needs repair.
2. The narrow comparison hides part of the second residence. Horizontal keyboard movement works, but the movement needs a visible cue and the first column must retain the comparison's meaning.
3. Keyboard commands do not focus search initially or place focus at the section selected. The palette promises fast inspection yet requires recovery steps. The same interaction evidence shows the sheet's expansion label changing without a change in height.

**Verdict:** The provenance and arithmetic foundation is strong; repair the displayed numerals and inspection controls before accepting the system.
