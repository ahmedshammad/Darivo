## Three delights

1. The [conversation chapter](../media/v2/design-en-dark-1440.png) gives the client's words priority. All six Brief fields are editable; the [recorded persona prompts](client-interactions.json) retain their complete wording and say explicitly that no live search was performed. A quiet demonstration is preferable to a false claim of understanding.
2. Recommendation specimens retain a reason and an honest trade-off at three sizes. The [Arabic sheet](../media/v2/signature-ar-sheet.png) keeps the same information hierarchy and returns focus to its trigger on Escape. These are reusable parts of a handoff, not a thumbnail-only listing.
3. Database identity is reloaded for protected access. A developer partner cannot inherit staff access or private inventory merely because of an old session or grant. The 40-test native run includes direct authorization and real-reference editing boundaries; preference updates cannot smuggle in a role change.

## Three problems

1. At 390px, the two-column Brief clips long purpose and settlement values. The underlying input retains them, but I cannot review the complete client requirement at a glance. The [mobile Brief](02-en-brief.png) needs more readable editing geometry.
2. The palette sends the viewport to Evidence while keyboard focus returns to the header. My next Tab starts at the wrong place. Search must receive initial focus and the destination must receive it after selection.
3. Expand sheet changes its accessible label without visibly expanding this specimen. A client should see the result of an action; use actual collapsed and expanded sizes with scrollable content.

**Verdict:** Clear contracts and privacy boundaries; the mobile Brief and keyboard journey need one more pass before this becomes the foundation for my daily work.

The existing matrix has no axe violations or overflow. These interaction defects demonstrate why that automated result alone is insufficient. Native-reader and physical-device review remain separate evidence requirements.
